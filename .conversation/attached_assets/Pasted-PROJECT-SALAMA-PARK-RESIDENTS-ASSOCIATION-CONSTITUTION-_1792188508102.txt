PROJECT: SALAMA PARK RESIDENTS ASSOCIATION — CONSTITUTION ADOPTION, MEMBERSHIP REGISTRATION & ONLINE VOTING PORTAL
1. YOUR ROLE
Act as a senior full-stack software engineer, UI/UX designer, database architect, cybersecurity specialist, and election-system developer.
Build a complete, production-oriented web application for the Salama Park Residents Association (SPRA), located in Zambia.
The platform will digitize the Association's Constitution adoption petition, establish a verified membership register, collect electronic declarations of consent, support an authorised Constitution adoption process, and facilitate the first election of office bearers and representatives.
This must be a functional full-stack application, not merely a landing page, static design, or clickable mockup.
Implement the frontend, backend, database, authentication, authorisation, validation, administrative workflows, reporting, and deployment configuration.
Do not leave critical functionality as non-working buttons or simulated success messages. If a third-party integration requires credentials, implement the integration interface, document the required environment variables, and clearly identify what remains to be configured.
2. TECHNOLOGY STACK
Use the following technologies unless the development environment requires a compatible alternative:

Frontend: React with TypeScript.
Styling: Tailwind CSS and a consistent, accessible component library.
Backend and database: Supabase with PostgreSQL.
Authentication: Supabase Auth or an equivalent secure authentication provider.

4. USER ROLES AND PERMISSIONS
Implement role-based access control with the following roles:
A. Public Visitor
Can:

View the homepage and public announcements.
Read or download approved versions of the Draft Constitution, amendment proposals, petition, and explanatory note.
View petition deadlines and official process updates.
View published aggregate statistics and authorised election results.
Read instructions about registration and voting.

Cannot:

View private resident records.
View NRC numbers, private contact details, individual signatures, or confidential verification documents.
Access member-only voting pages.

B. Resident / Applicant
Can:

Create an account.
Submit a membership registration application.
Provide required residency information.
Read the Constitution and petition.
Submit an electronic declaration of consent to the entire Draft Constitution.
View their own application status and confirmation reference.
Correct or update their information through an approved process.
Request withdrawal of their petition signature before the applicable closing deadline.
Receive notifications concerning their application.
Vote when authorised and eligible.
Receive a ballot receipt code that does not reveal their selections.
View their own voting confirmation without accessing their ballot contents.

Cannot:

Approve their own membership.
Change their eligibility or voting status.
Vote more than once in the same election.
View another resident's private information.
Change submitted election results.

C. Returning Officer
Can:

Review registrations and petition submissions.
Verify eligibility according to the formally approved membership criteria.
Record approval, rejection, or a request for clarification.
Provide reasons for decisions.
Review duplicate and disputed entries.
Maintain the verification record.
Monitor petition thresholds and election turnout.
Prepare verification reports.
Publish results only after the required authorisation.
Export authorised reports.

Cannot:

Contest an election while serving as Returning Officer.
Alter or delete a cast ballot.
See which candidate or option an identified resident selected.
Change an election's rules after voting begins.

D. Scrutineer
Can:

Review authorised verification records.
Assist with duplicate and disputed-entry reviews.
Independently review counts and audit reports.
Record objections and findings.

Restrict access to personal information to the minimum required for the assigned duty.
E. Administrator / Executive Committee
Can:

Configure the portal.
Upload approved governance documents.
Set petition and election dates.
Configure membership requirements following formal approval.
Appoint authorised officials and assign roles.
Approve online voting arrangements and record the relevant resolution.
Configure election positions, candidates, voting periods, and approved eligibility rules.
Review audit logs.
Authorise publication of verified registers and results.
Export reports and manage official announcements.

Use separation of duties for critical actions. Where feasible, require two authorised officials to approve changes to election configuration, publication of final results, and other sensitive operations.
An administrator must not have an interface or database privilege that exposes the relationship between a resident's identity and their secret ballot.

10. PETITION THRESHOLDS AND CALCULATIONS

Minimum 25% quorum: ceiling of N × 0.25.

Do not invent candidates or publish unapproved candidate information.
Voting security requirements

Only eligible members in the frozen voter snapshot may vote.
Each eligible member may cast only one ballot per election.
Duplicate submissions must be prevented server-side and through database constraints.
Ballot contents must remain secret.
Resident identities and vote selections must be stored separately.
No ordinary administrator, Returning Officer, scrutineer, or database user should be able to view an identity-to-vote mapping.
The system must issue a random ballot receipt code.
The receipt code must confirm inclusion in the ballot system without revealing the voter's selections.
Ballots must be immutable after submission, except through a formally defined and audited election cancellation or restart procedure.
Closing an election must prevent new submissions.
The system must preserve audit logs for opening, closing, configuration changes, counting and publication.
The counting method must be documented and reproducible.
Published results must include the number of eligible voters, ballots cast, turnout, valid ballots, invalid ballots where applicable, abstentions where applicable, and candidate or option totals.
Provide an independent verification and reconciliation procedure.
If the system cannot provide the required secrecy, integrity, or auditability, do not describe it as suitable for official elections.

Implement appropriate cryptographic separation between voter authentication and ballot storage. Do not merely hide names from the interface while retaining an easily accessible table that maps each voter to their choices.
Explain the limits of the chosen design and have the system independently reviewed before it is used for a binding election.
Voting user experience
The voting interface should:

Explain the ballot before the voter begins.
Display only positions and candidates relevant to the election.
Allow the voter to review their selections before submission.
Clearly warn that submission is final.
Require explicit confirmation before casting the ballot.
Display a receipt reference after a successful submission.
Prevent accidental duplicate submissions.
Work reliably on mobile devices and slow internet connections.

Do not show an individual's selections in their voting history or confirmation receipt.
